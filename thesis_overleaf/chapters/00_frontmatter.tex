% ---------- Abstract ----------
\frontheading{Abstract}
{\setstretch{1.3}
Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) gives a language model passages to answer from, but the
model can still give answers that those passages do not support. This project measures how
retrieval quality affects such hallucination for two small open-weight language models, and
tests a rule that needs no model training: withhold the answer when a confidence signal is low.
Four retrievers, a closed-book condition and an oracle condition are compared on
\val{data.eval_questions} multi-hop HotpotQA questions over \val{data.passages} passages. With
both gold passages in the top five, 7.0\,\% and 4.5\,\% of the answers are hallucinated; with only
one, 63.1\,\% and 77.7\,\%. Differences between retrievers are smaller and their intervals overlap.
The score of the top passage does not work as a signal (AUROC 0.46--0.54). A check of whether the
answer's words appear in the passages does better, lowering hallucination from 21.3\,\% to 11.0\,\%
at 74.9\,\% coverage in the best case, at a cost in correct answers. A human check of 100 answers
agrees only moderately with the language-model judge ($\kappa=0.54$), so absolute rates may be
too high, but it preserves the gap.\par}
