\chapter{Problem Statement}\label{chap:problem}

\begin{itemize}
\item A RAG system can give answers that its retrieved passages do not support. It is not clear
  how strongly this depends on the \textbf{quality of retrieval} when the generator is a
  \textbf{small open-weight model}.
\item Existing ways to reduce such answers, such as Self-RAG and CRAG, need \textbf{fine-tuning}.
  It is unclear how much can be achieved with a rule that needs no training and works with any
  model behind an API.
\item This thesis compares \textbf{six conditions} (closed-book, BM25, dense, hybrid, hybrid with
  reranking, oracle) for \textbf{two generators} on \textbf{multi-hop HotpotQA questions}, and
  evaluates a rule that abstains when a \textbf{confidence signal is low}: the score of a retrieved
  passage, or the share of the answer's words found in the passages.
\item \textbf{Research questions:}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{RQ1:} How does retrieval quality affect hallucination (faithfulness) in RAG with
    small open-weight LLMs?
  \item \textbf{RQ2:} Can a training-free rule that abstains when a confidence signal is low
    reduce hallucination without losing many correct answers?
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Contribution:} unlike Self-RAG and CRAG, the rule studied here needs no fine-tuning
  and works with any API model, and its gain and cost are both measured on held-out questions.
\end{itemize}
